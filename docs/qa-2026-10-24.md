# Regression and ease-of-use review, 2026-10-24 (QA-01)

**Status:** two passes done; fixes landed with QA-01 and QA-02. **Revision history:** 2026-10-24
first version; 2026-10-24 second pass (QA-02) for people who aren't comfortable with computers at all.

Two questions: does everything a user does still work end to end, and can someone new to
computers and to digitizing (we pictured an older woman who sews, using a laptop) get from
nothing to a file on her machine's USB stick without help?

## 1. Regression: the main workflows, end to end

Run through `stitchfoundry-cli` (the same commands the app runs). Every machine file was read
back by an independent reader (pyembroidery) and its stitches counted.

| Area | Checks | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Samples on Home (all 9) open, have stitches and fit their hoop | 9 | all pass |
| Save in every machine format (DST, EXP, JEF, PES, PEC, VP3, XXX, U01, PCS, TAP, DSB, HUS, SEW), read back by pyembroidery with the same stitch count | 13 | all pass (VP3 and SEW differ by 1–2 stitches: tie-ins counted differently) |
| Open machine files StitchFoundry wrote (DST, PES, JEF, EXP, VP3) | 5 | all pass |
| Make a Design: words, a shape, an appliqué, a photo with a border, a sample, an in-the-hoop coaster (large, scalloped), a quilt block | 7 | 3 passed, **4 failed** (fixed, below) |
| Every in-the-hoop project (14) made and fits its hoop | 14 | all pass |
| Save for a memory card, sew-out GIF and MP4, Check Design | 4 | all pass |
| Undo after Make a Design | 1 | **failed** (same cause) |
| **Total** | **53** | **first run 48 passed, 5 failed; after the fixes 53 passed** |

**Two bugs found.**

1. Make a Design with no hoop chosen and no My Machine set stopped with "`hoop` is a hoop's
   name or {width, height, shape?}". Anyone who skipped choosing a machine and went straight to
   words, a shape, an appliqué or a picture hit it. Fixed: such a design gets the everyday 5×7
   hoop (Brother 130 × 180), and a test covers it (`embroidery_my_machine.rs`).
2. Found on the rerun: "Grandma" at 25 mm tall came out about 145 mm long, wider than the 5×7's
   130 mm, and Make a Design left it hanging over the hoop's edge. Fixed: words too long for the
   hoop are made smaller to nine tenths of it, and the guide's next step says so ("The words
   were made smaller (81%) to fit in the hoop.": 117 mm long, 23 mm tall). Tests: `words_too_long_for_the_hoop_are_made_smaller_to_fit`,
   `the_guide_says_when_words_were_made_smaller`.

**After the fixes: 53 of 53 pass.**

## 2. Ease of use, for someone new

Looked at in the app at 1440 × 900 (a common laptop size), in simple mode ("New to
embroidery"), at each text size. Ranked by how badly each one stops her.

| # | What she meets | Why it matters | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At the biggest text size, windows taller than the screen (My Machine, Make a Design) put **Done** and **Next** below the screen's edge | She can't finish, and doesn't know why | **Fixed**: the window's contents scroll with a visible bar; Done, Back and Next always show |
| 2 | Save for My Machine, with no machine chosen yet, opens My Machine with no word of why; after choosing, nothing saves | She thinks the save failed | **Fixed**: the window says "First tell StitchFoundry which machine you have… Then press Done to save", and Done goes on to save |
| 3 | Thread colours listed as codes: "1. #ffffff", "4. #4a90c8" (photo designs, Your Design's Steps, Sequence) | Meaningless; she can't match it to a spool | **Fixed**: a thread without a name is called after the nearest thread colour ("White", "Sky Blue", "Old Gold"), translated |
| 4 | At the biggest text size the column of buttons runs under Home and Text size, overlapping | Buttons hidden under others | **Fixed**: the buttons scroll with a visible bar above the bottom links |
| 5 | Text size is small letters in a corner | The one control she needs most is hard to find | **Better**: bigger letters and label; still at the bottom of the column |
| 6 | Home says "Machine embroidery digitizing — fast, open, scriptable." | Jargon | **Fixed**: "Turn your words, shapes and pictures into designs for your embroidery machine." |
| 7 | The hint bar says "Shift+Click to select multiple objects \| Alt+Drag the object to duplicate" | Modifier keys and jargon | **Fixed in simple mode**: "Click a part of the design to choose it \| Drag it to move it" |
| 8 | Dark theme, grey-on-dark text | Low contrast for older eyes | **Fixed (QA-02)**: Light or dark screen, next to Text size |
| 9 | A photo with default settings can come out heavy: the macaw was 85,580 stitches, 26 colours, 4½ hours of sewing | A beginner's first photo ties up her machine for an afternoon | **Better (QA-02)**: above 30,000 stitches Make a Design says "This is a big design: about 4 h 31 min of sewing. A picture with fewer colours sews more quickly." Still to do: a one-press "Make it simpler" |
| 10 | The floating Embroidery panel (full mode) sits over the design | Covers what she's working on | **Not yet** (full mode only; simple mode has no floating panel) |

What already works well for her: Make a Design's big cards with a plain line under each, simple
mode's large labelled buttons, Start Over asking first, Keep Safe saving on its own, Undo and
Redo side by side, and Watch It Sew.

## 3. Second pass: someone who isn't comfortable with computers at all

The first pass assumed she could find her way round a computer. The second assumes she can't:
folder dialogs, zoom percentages, file names with extensions, options dialogs and hover-only
help all stop her.

| # | What she meets | Now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Save for My Machine asks her to find her USB stick in a folder dialog | **Fixed**: StitchFoundry looks for the USB sticks plugged in (macOS, Windows, Linux). One: the design goes straight on it. None: "Plug in the USB stick you take to your machine. Wait a moment, then press Look Again." Several: one big button each, by name ("Save on KINGSTON (E:)"). "Save on This Computer Instead…" stays for the rest |
| 2 | Save for My Machine, the one thing every design ends with, scrolls out of sight at the bigger text sizes | **Fixed**: it stays in view above the column's foot |
| 3 | The bottom bar shows "173.84 %", "0°", "1 Artboard 1", coordinates and timings | **Fixed**: in simple mode it shows See It All, Closer, Farther and the last message, large |
| 4 | Add a Picture opens a file dialog wherever it was last, then a Place dialog of options (link, template, replace) | **Fixed**: the dialog opens in her Pictures folder, takes iPhone photos (HEIC), and the picture goes straight in |
| 5 | Threads called after our own chart's codes ("G02 Red") | **Fixed**: "Red" (brand codes such as Madeira's stay: they help buy the spool) |
| 6 | Sewing time "0:07:12" | **Fixed**: "7 min", "4 h 31 min" |
| 7 | Dark screen only, unless she finds Preferences | **Fixed**: Light or dark screen in the column |

Still open (proposed next):
- Help that only shows when the pointer rests on a button: show a short line under each button
  instead, or a "What's this?" mode.
- The menus (File, Edit, View, Help) are still there in simple mode; most of what they hold is
  for experienced users.
- Light could be the first screen for simple mode (the owner's call).
- A printed one-page "How to" with pictures, from the same screenshots, to keep beside the
  machine.

## 4. How to repeat it

- Regression: `python3 e2e.py <stitchfoundry-cli> <out-dir> <photo>` (kept with the session
  notes; it drives `stitchfoundry-cli run` and pyembroidery).
- Ease of use: run the app with `--control`, switch to simple mode (`window.experience
  {level: "beginner"}`), set `prefs.set {key: "uiScaling", value: 1.5}`, open each window and
  take `ui.screenshot`.
- The fixes have tests: `simple_mode_names_threads_and_says_why_my_machine_opened`,
  `hints_name_keys_as_the_menus_do`, `a_thread_without_a_name_is_called_after_the_nearest_generic_colour`,
  the new-design hoop test in `embroidery_my_machine.rs`; QA-02: `save_for_my_machine_finds_the_usb_stick`,
  `simple_mode_status_bar_and_times_are_plain`, `the_usb_sticks_plugged_in` (the app's `eject.rs`).
